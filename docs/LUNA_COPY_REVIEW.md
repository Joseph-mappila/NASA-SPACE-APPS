# LUNA copy and science review

Status: Reviewed. The narrative decision was to keep the original prose and values, correcting only spelling, punctuation, and formatting. The broader proposals below were not approved and are not used in the game.

The Luna setting, HAL, 15-person crew, Day 57 sequence, two opening objectives, mission order, pass/fail outcomes, and downstream consequences are unchanged.

## Prologue

### 1. Lightning

**Original**

> Lightning, a phenomenon we now consider with fascination and understanding was once a source of great fear to humans.
> They couldn't understand what caused the big flash across the sky, and the big bang that followed it.
> It was nothing like they had seen before, never known before.
> They attributed it to gods, they worshipped nature begging for forgiveness for their sins.

**Proposed**

> Today we can study lightning. Long ago, its sudden flash and rolling thunder frightened people because they did not know what caused them. Different communities told stories to explain the storm—but curiosity kept us looking for answers.

**Why**

- Keeps fear, mystery, and curiosity without making one claim about every early culture.
- Names thunder separately from lightning.
- Uses shorter sentences for ages 7–12.

### 2. Fire

**Original**

> Suddenly the lightning hit a tree and engulfed it in fire, and they were fascinated by it. Scared by it.
> But curiosity pulled them forward and eventually they learnt to use it, they learnt to hunt with it.

**Proposed**

> Lightning could set a tree ablaze. Fire was dangerous, but curiosity drew people closer. Over many generations, humans learned to keep fire alive and use it for warmth, light, protection, and cooking.

**Why**

- Presents the scene as a possible route to natural fire, not one known historical event.
- Replaces the vague “hunt with it” claim with well-supported uses.
- Preserves fear giving way to curiosity.

### 3. Ore and metal

**Original**

> They found that the rocks in their sorroundings were not the same, some were brittle, some however were shiny and sonorous.
> They used these and found that with fire they could be moulded into weapons, into structures far stronger than anything they had thought of before.

**Proposed**

> People noticed that the rocks around them were not all alike. Some crumbled. Others shone or rang when struck. They discovered that some rocks held metal-bearing ore. With heat and hammering, metal could be shaped into strong tools and structures.

**Why**

- Corrects “sorroundings.”
- Explains “sonorous” in language children can understand.
- Distinguishes ore from finished metal.
- Keeps forging central and avoids making weapons the lesson’s focus.

### 4. Transport and spaceflight

**Original**

> From simple wheel driven carts to cars, to ships and to planes. And finally to rockets, we finally managed to leave the little speck of dust we were confined to in this galaxy.

**Proposed**

> From wheel-driven carts came new ways to travel: cars, ships, and planes. Rockets finally carried humans beyond our home world—but we are still only beginning to explore our corner of the galaxy.

**Why**

- Humans have not escaped or explored the galaxy.
- Preserves the exact cart → car → ship → plane → rocket sequence.
- Removes the repeated “finally.”

### 5. Title

**Original**

> Throughout all this, one thing remained consistent, the curiosity of mankind that set them apart.
> Which is why CurioCET presents:
> Luna

**Proposed**

> Through every step, one thing remained: human curiosity.
> CurioCET presents
> LUNA

**Why**

- Preserves the meaning and title while making the line inclusive and cinematic.

## Day 57 briefing

### 6. Welcome and crew status

**Original**

> Welcome to Luna, the lunar outpost set up by NASA on the moon!
> HAL: Good morning captain. We are currently day 57 of mission Luna. Current systems are all operational.
> Crew: 15/15 alive and well. No casualties to report.

**Proposed**

> Welcome to Luna, a fictional lunar outpost inspired by NASA research.
> HAL: Good morning, Captain [player name]. This is Day 57 of Mission Luna. Core systems are operating.
> Crew: 15 of 15 are safe and well.

**Why**

- NASA does not currently operate a crewed lunar outpost.
- Personalizes the briefing without changing the player’s role.
- Avoids the unnatural contradiction between “all operational” and Power at 52%.
- Keeps the full crew count without an unnecessarily grim casualty line.

### 7. Cabin atmosphere

**Original**

> Oxygen: Normal (PIO2: 150 mmHg)

**Proposed**

> Cabin air: Normal · 8.2 psia · 34% oxygen
>
> Optional explanation: NASA has studied this lower-pressure “exploration atmosphere” for future Moon and Mars missions because it can shorten preparation for spacewalks. Its inspired oxygen pressure is about 128 mmHg.

**Why**

- NASA’s current exploration-atmosphere reference gives 8.2 psia, 34% oxygen, and inspired oxygen pressure around 128 mmHg—not 145–155 or 150 mmHg.
- Keeps the real value in an optional explanation instead of making children decode unexplained notation.

**Primary source**

- NASA Technical Reports Server, “Moon to Mars (M2M): Exploration Atmosphere” (2024): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240003211

## Mission 1: Electrodynamic Dust Shield

### 8. EDS explanation and result

**Original**

> NASA Kennedy Space Center's Electrodynamic Dust Shield (EDS) uses transparent Indium Tin Oxide (ITO) electrodes to send a traveling AC electric wave across the glass, creating a dielectrophoretic force (F ∝ V² / d³) that lifts and bounces dust off without physical contact.
>
> Pass: Array returns to 100% efficiency; outpost batteries charge to 100% before the flare arrives.

**Proposed**

> NASA’s Electrodynamic Dust Shield uses transparent electrodes to create a moving electric field. The field lifts charged dust and carries it off protected surfaces without scrubbing the glass.
>
> Pass: The array returns to its full rated output, and the outpost batteries can charge to 100% before the solar event.

**Why**

- NASA sources support transparent electrodes, electric fields, and active dust removal.
- The exact force formula in the draft is not established by the cited public overview and is unnecessary for the lesson.
- “100% efficiency” incorrectly implies perfect conversion of sunlight to electricity.
- The game’s brush remains limited to clumps on the outer bezel; it never brushes the solar coverglass.

**Primary sources**

- NASA Kennedy Space Center, “NASA Technology Helps Guard Against Lunar Dust”: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/kennedy/nasa-technology-helps-guard-against-lunar-dust/
- NASA Technology Transfer, “Self-Cleaning Coatings for Space or Earth”: https://technology.nasa.gov/patent/KSC-TOPS-99

## Mission 2: Trash Compaction and Processing System

### 9. TCPS explanation

**Original**

> NASA's TCPS applies controlled heat and pneumatic compression to convert mixed trash and wet wipes into sterile, dense tiles (375 kg/m³) while recovering up to 98% of trapped water. Heating must drive water activity (aw) below 0.6 so bacteria and mold cannot grow.

**Proposed**

> NASA’s Trash Compaction and Processing System uses heat and pressure to squeeze water from mixed trash and compress the dry material into dense, stable tiles. The recovered water must still be treated before the crew can use it. The tile’s water activity must be 0.5 or lower to prevent microbial growth during storage.

**Why**

- NASA describes heat and pressure, but not specifically pneumatic compression in the reviewed overview.
- Reviewed NASA requirements set water activity at no more than 0.5, not below 0.6.
- “Up to 98%” varies by test and trash model; “recovers water” is accurate without presenting a best-case result as guaranteed.
- TCPS safens waste; “sterile” is a stronger claim.
- The 375 kg/m³ tile-density requirement is valid but belongs in the optional technical detail.

**Optional technical detail**

> NASA test requirements call for stable tiles with a density of at least 375 kg/m³. Ground-test processing uses at least 150°C for at least 3.5 hours. The game compresses that long process into a short control simulation.

**Primary sources**

- NASA Ames, “The Trash Compaction Processing System”: https://www.nasa.gov/ames/space-biosciences/bioengineering-branch/the-trash-compaction-processing-system/
- NASA Technical Reports Server, “TCPS Technology Demonstrations Science Objectives and Requirement Definitions”: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220017801/downloads/ICES_2023_207_15_8.pdf

### 10. Mission 2 outcome

**Original**

> Pass: Reclaims +15% Water and produces 6 dense polyethylene/waste tiles (hydrogen-rich radiation blocks).

**Proposed**

> Pass: Luna’s scenario recovers 15% of the outpost’s water reserve and produces six dense waste tiles. Their plastic-rich material can add useful mass around the storm shelter.

**Why**

- Clearly marks the numbers as game-state outcomes, not universal TCPS performance.
- Avoids implying that every mixed-waste tile has a fixed composition or certified shielding value.
- Preserves the exact +15% water and six-tile consequences required by later missions.

# Narrative Bible

This document defines structure and constraints. The narrative lead owns the final prose and should not treat placeholder wording as finished dialogue.

## Working premise

An astronaut at Aster Outpost is temporarily isolated after the return rover fails. A rescue rover can arrive in three sols if the outpost remains habitable and its beacon stays operational. The player joins as a Junior Mission Controller.

## Audience and reading level

- Primary audience: ages 7–12.
- Keep individual speech/text cards short enough to read in a few seconds.
- Prefer concrete verbs and sensory details over technical exposition.
- Define technical terms in context.
- Never talk down to the player.

## Tone

- Wonder before fear.
- Tension without horror.
- Competence, cooperation, and curiosity.
- The astronaut trusts the player and explains consequences honestly.
- Humor may release tension but should not imitate *The Martian*.

## Originality boundary

A stranded astronaut on Mars is a general premise. Do not reproduce recognizable material from *The Martian*:

- Mark Watney or a similar personality;
- potatoes as the central crop;
- human-waste farming;
- Pathfinder communication recovery;
- copied rescue beats, jokes, dialogue, suits, typography, or compositions;
- “science the…” phrasing used as a marketing hook.

Use NASA-backed lettuce or chile-pepper research instead.

## Character placeholders

### The astronaut

- Name: to be chosen by narrative lead.
- Role: systems scientist or habitat engineer.
- Voice: calm, warm, occasionally vulnerable, never reckless.
- Visual consistency: one suit, one face/reference, one color accent.

### Mission companion

- Optional compact robot or habitat AI.
- Purpose: tutorials, alerts, accessibility prompts.
- Not a generative chatbot.
- Voice: concise and practical; does not replace the astronaut emotionally.

### The player

- Addressed as “Mission Controller” or a chosen callsign.
- No account, profile, age, or personal data collection.

## Story rhythm

Use this repeated pattern:

```text
quiet image
→ small personal detail
→ system warning
→ player decision
→ tactile action
→ visible consequence
→ reflection
→ NASA source
```

Each chapter should contain one calm moment before its crisis so the experience does not feel like constant alarms.

## Chapter writing worksheet

For every chapter, complete:

```text

```

## Prologue beats

1. Establish Mars as beautiful but demanding.
2. Introduce Aster Outpost and its purpose.
3. Reveal the failed return rover.
4. Establish rescue timing.
5. Invite the player into Mission Control.
6. Show the four resources without explaining everything at once.

## Chapter 1 beats

1. Show the mature greenhouse as a source of comfort.
2. Reveal reduced recycler output.
3. Explain that crew reserve and crops need the same limited water.
4. Let the player choose and adjust irrigation.
5. Change the greenhouse illustration to reflect the result.
6. Explain that food grown in space still requires careful environmental control.

## Chapter 2 beats

1. Begin with a quiet transmission or greenhouse moment.
2. Introduce the archived NASA space-weather warning.
3. Move the astronaut into a physically shielded shelter.
4. Let the player decide which electrical loads to shed.
5. Perform routing/hatch interactions.
6. Show the storm passing and the sacrificed system.

## Chapter 3 beats

1. Show the consequences carried from earlier decisions.
2. Dust-reduced solar generation and an electrical fault cause darkness.
3. The rescue beacon, greenhouse heat, and oxygen recycling compete for power.
4. Perform the flashlight repair.
5. Allocate recovered power.
6. Resolve rescue and summarize the chain of decisions.

## Epilogue rules

- Celebrate reasoning, not a numeric high score alone.
- Mention one sacrifice and one achievement.
- End with agency: engineering is choosing, testing, learning, and trying again.
- Offer replay and a source/mission-log view.

## Story content checklist

- [ ] No text baked into illustration files.
- [ ] Every technical word is explained.
- [ ] No crop grows unrealistically fast.
- [ ] No storm behavior is exaggerated into Earth-like wind force.
- [ ] Radiation shelter is physical, not magical.
- [ ] No copied movie dialogue or visual identity.
- [ ] All spoken content has captions.
- [ ] Every crisis leads to a decision, not only a minigame.
